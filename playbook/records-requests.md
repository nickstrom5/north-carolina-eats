# Public-records request drafts (North Carolina) — not sent

Nothing here has been sent. Each request goes out only with Nick's explicit yes, from his own email. North Carolina's Public Records
Act (G.S. Chapter 132) lets **any person** ask, for any reason (unlike Tennessee's citizens-only act), and an agency may not ask why.
Agencies may charge the actual cost of copying or extraction and say so before they fill a request. Confirm each address before sending:
the DHHS ones come from a DHHS data-sharing guidebook and the Office of Communications page (found 2026-10-26).

## 1. NC DHHS, Environmental Health Section: statewide food-establishment inspections

To: public.records@dhhs.nc.gov (cc news@dhhs.nc.gov, the Office of Communications, which handles public-records requests;
mail: NC DHHS Office of Communications, 2001 Mail Service Center, Raleigh, NC 27699-2000)

Subject: Public records request: food establishment inspection scores, all counties (G.S. 132)

> Under North Carolina's Public Records Act (G.S. Chapter 132), I request an electronic copy of the food-establishment inspection
> records that local health departments report to the Division of Public Health through the statewide environmental health system
> (the records shown on public.cdpehs.com, "View Inspections").
>
> For each establishment permitted under 15A NCAC 18A .2600 (restaurants, food stands, mobile food units, push carts and limited food
> service establishments), please include: State ID number, county, establishment name, street address, city, ZIP, establishment
> type, and permit status; and for each inspection since January 1, 2023: inspection date, inspection type (routine, re-inspection,
> complaint), final score and grade. If violation items are kept in the same system, please include the item number, Food Code
> section and points deducted for each inspection. Inspector names and permit-holder personal information are not needed.
>
> A CSV or Excel file is ideal; any machine-readable format is fine. If there is a cost, please tell me before filling the request.
> If some counties' records (for example Union, Halifax or Swain) are not in the state system, I'd be grateful to know which, so I can
> ask those counties directly.
>
> Thank you,
> Nick Soderstrom · nick@eatsranked.com

Why: only Wake County publishes inspections as data; the other 99 counties' scores and grades are search-only. The app would show each
place's official score and grade card, labeled official, with the date (as it does for Wake).
