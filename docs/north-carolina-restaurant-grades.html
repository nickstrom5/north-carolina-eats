North Carolina restaurant grades, explained

A North Carolina restaurant hangs its grade card from the county health department where customers see it on the way in, as the state's rules require, and a new card goes up after every inspection. The letter comes from the inspection score under a state rule, 15A NCAC 18A .2662: A for a score of 90 or more, B for 80 to under 90, C for 70 to under 80. Below 70, the permit is revoked.

Wake County's scores

Wake County is the one county that publishes its food service permits and inspections as open data. For 2,097 Wake County places we list, the app and the web search show the latest score and the letter it earns, the date, earlier inspections and the items the inspector cited, in the county's words. Records through September 14, 2026.

At their latest inspection, by the county's score:

  • A 2,087 places
  • B 10 places
  • C 0 places

The letter is the one rule .2662 assigns to the county's own score, so it matches the card by the door; North Carolina Eats computes no grade of its own, and a score the county didn't publish shows as a dash. Points come off for each Food Code item the inspector finds out of compliance. One inspection is a snapshot of one day. No page here lists places by score; the web search can sort Wake's list by it.
