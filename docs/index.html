Built around how North Carolina eats

Star ratings won't tell you which pit still cooks the whole hog, or where the slaw comes red. North Carolina Eats is organized the way people here eat: Eastern whole hog barbecue, Lexington barbecue, Carolina hot dogs, Calabash seafood and biscuits and livermush, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Wake County places carry the county's own inspection score and grade, every other place links its county's reports, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out in the mountains or on the Outer Banks.

What stays on your phone

North Carolina Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is North Carolina Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The Eastern whole hog, Lexington barbecue, Carolina hot dog, Calabash seafood and biscuit and livermush lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: its website or menu, a dated local news story, a Wake County inspection or an official tourism listing. The wider directory of 22,903 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against Wake County's permit list, and adds Wake restaurants from that list.

Where can I get Eastern and Lexington barbecue?

Our Eastern list has 9 hand-checked whole hog places in 5 towns, and our Lexington list 13 Piedmont pits in 6 towns. In the web search, either list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do North Carolina restaurant grades work?

Every county inspects restaurants under the state's rules and posts a grade card by the door: A for a score of 90 or more, B for 80 to under 90, C for 70 to under 80. Wake County publishes its inspections as data, so Wake places show the latest official score, its grade, the date and the items cited. The other counties' reports are in the state's inspection search, linked from each place; we copy no score and compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. North Carolina's ABC permits aren't published as a list, so alcohol shows as unknown everywhere, never as no.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
